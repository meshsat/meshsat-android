MeshSat turns an Android phone into a MeshSat gateway. Pair it with a MeshSat node, a pocket-sized box with a Meshtastic LoRa radio and a RockBLOCK 9603 Iridium modem, and one phone can send and receive over the mesh, by satellite and by SMS. It keeps working when the mobile network and the internet are gone. It also works with any plain Meshtastic radio, for the mesh only.

Status: pre-release. This is a prototype under active development. It has never been deployed to a real user and has never been used in an actual emergency. Do not rely on it for your safety.

What it does:

• Mesh: Meshtastic over Bluetooth LE with the official protobufs: text, positions, telemetry, waypoints, node info and traceroute. A reply to a node goes to that node, not to the whole channel.
• Satellite: Iridium SBD through the node's RockBLOCK 9603, up to 340 bytes out and 270 bytes in. Messages wait in a queue and are retried until they go out. The app opens a satellite session only when there is something to send, when the modem rings or when you check the mailbox, never on a timer, because every session costs a credit. Passes are predicted on the phone, with no internet needed.
• SMS through the phone's own SIM, optionally encrypted per conversation with AES-256-GCM. Messages are compressed with MSVQ-SC, which is lossy: what arrives means the same, but may not be word for word what was sent.
• APRS through a KISS TNC over TCP (Direwolf, for example) or directly to APRS-IS.
• Hub, optional: the phone reports to a MeshSat Hub over MQTT with a client certificate and takes remote commands. The Hub is open source and can be self-hosted.
• TAK, through the Hub: your team's TAK picture appears on the map, and with Enable TAK on the phone sends its position, an SOS and its chat to your TAK server.
• Reticulum: the phone runs as a Reticulum transport node and relays between its links. A message to a Reticulum contact can carry a voice note, or a photo picked from your gallery; both travel on a Reticulum link only.
• Safety: hold the SOS button for 3 seconds and the SOS goes out on every route the phone has, each retried until it is sent, until you cancel it. A check-in timer and zones drawn on the map.
• The map works offline down to country level. Night mode turns the whole app red.

Satellite messaging needs a RockBLOCK modem and Iridium credits from a provider; Iridium is a paid commercial network.

Internet connections: the bundled world map covers country level; closer in, map tiles come from OpenStreetMap's tile servers while you look at the map online. Once a satellite modem is set up (the MeshSat node's modem seen, or a RockBLOCK 9704 connected), Iridium orbit data is downloaded from celestrak.org, or tle.ivanstanojevic.me if that fails, when the stored copy is more than a day old, checked at most every 12 hours; before that only when you tap Update on the passes screen. The Hub, APRS-IS and Reticulum over TCP connect only when you set them up, and a field kit you add under Setup is reached at the address you give it, or through the Hub relay. A voice note's transcript, offered for routes that carry text only, comes from the phone's own speech recogniser, which may use its maker's servers; MeshSat itself sends the recording nowhere but to the contact. Node firmware is downloaded from MeshSat's releases on github.com only when you tap Check for update. Nothing else goes online.

Permissions: Bluetooth and location, because Android needs location to scan for Bluetooth devices and the map shows where you are; SMS, to send and receive through your SIM; the microphone, only while you hold the voice note button; notifications. Starting after a phone restart is off until you switch it on.

Documentation, and a table of what has and has not been verified: https://docs.meshsat.net/android/
